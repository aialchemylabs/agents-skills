## 1. Evidence Basis

Use the following sources as research foundations, not as affiliation or certification claims.

### Source-supported principles

- Harvard Business School's public Entrepreneurship Essentials material describes evaluating people, opportunity, context, and deal; using structured experiments to validate ideas; managing risk and reward; and making decisions as conditions change. Sources: [HBS Online, Entrepreneurship Essentials syllabus](https://online.hbs.edu/documents/Syllabus_Entrepreneurship_Essentials.pdf) and [HBS Newsroom, Entrepreneurship Essentials launch](https://www.hbs.edu/news/releases/hbx-launches-entrepreneurship-essentials).
- HBS faculty guidance on business plans emphasizes the people, opportunity, context, and risk-reward questions and warns that detailed new-venture financial projections are highly uncertain. Source: [HBS Working Knowledge, How to Write a Great Business Plan](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-to-write-a-great-business-plan).
- The U.S. National Science Foundation describes customer discovery, hypothesis testing, validation, pivoting, and minimum viable products as elements of evidence-based entrepreneurial learning. Source: [NSF, I-Corps Biennial Report 2023](https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/2023-06/TIP_I-CorpsReport_2023_Final_6.21.2023.508.pdf).
- MIT's entrepreneurship program describes identifying and quantifying opportunities, developing value propositions, understanding customers, considering legal and ethical issues, and working through financial and team questions. Source: [MIT Martin Trust Center, New Enterprises](https://entrepreneurship.mit.edu/an-inside-look-at-new-enterprises/).
- Strategyzer's professional guidance recommends identifying critical desirability, feasibility, and viability assumptions, connecting experiments to hypotheses, and using stronger evidence when constraints allow. Sources: [Strategyzer, Start with the most critical hypotheses](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/how-to-test-your-idea-start-with-the-most-critical-hypotheses) and [Strategyzer, Select the next best test](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/how-to-select-the-next-best-test-from-the-experiment-library).

These sources support multidimensional venture evaluation and staged experimentation. They do not provide a deterministic formula for venture success.

## 2. Operational Design Choices

The following are design judgments for this skill:

- Separate broad opportunity discovery, primary customer research, early venture shaping, and mature-plan review.
- Add feasibility, responsibility, and adaptability explicitly to the people-opportunity-context-business arrangement evaluation.
- Calibrate evidence requirements to stage and the reversibility of the proposed commitment.
- Define an MVP by the decision and evidence it can produce, not by minimal feature count.
- Use exactly one current decision among `Build`, `Test`, `Pivot`, `Pause`, and `Kill`.
- Refuse indefinite experimentation when the expected decision value of more evidence is below its cost or downside.

Adapt the model to the venture and do not present it as a prediction engine.

## 3. Stage Calibration

| Stage | Primary question | Appropriate evidence | Premature demand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concept | Is there a specific problem and reachable customer worth investigating? | Problem signals, founder access, early interviews | Multi-year forecast or scale plan |
| Discovery | How does the customer behave, decide, and solve it now? | Recent behavior, observation, alternatives, buying system | Product roadmap before problem clarity |
| Validation | Will customers commit, pay, adopt, and receive value? | Prototype behavior, pre-order, paid pilot, delivered outcome | Treating praise as traction |
| Early traction | Does use repeat with coherent economics? | Revenue quality, retention, cohort behavior, delivery cost | Extrapolating from one bespoke customer |
| Repeatability | Can acquisition and delivery repeat without unacceptable risk? | Channel performance, payback, retention, quality, capacity | Scaling before controls and constraints are understood |

Increase evidence strength as the commitment becomes less reversible.

## 4. Venture Evidence Grid

| Dimension | Questions | Evidence | Unknown or contradiction | Consequence | Next test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| People | Access, capability, incentives, capacity, trust, gaps | Source anchors | Material gap | Effect on execution | Smallest response |
| Opportunity | Customer, problem, alternatives, buying, reach, retention | Source anchors | Material gap | Effect on demand | Smallest response |
| Context | Timing, regulation, technology, ecosystem, incumbent response | Source anchors | Material gap | Effect on timing or downside | Smallest response |
| Business model | Payer, price, channel, costs, cash, capital, arrangements | Source anchors | Material gap | Effect on viability | Smallest response |
| Feasibility | Delivery, quality, privacy, safety, operations, compliance | Source anchors | Material gap | Effect on responsibility | Specialist or experiment |

Avoid averaging a fatal weakness into a respectable total score. State the interaction among dimensions; a good opportunity with misaligned deal terms can still be unattractive.

## 5. Coherent Venture Model

### Venture thesis

```markdown
For <specific customer in context>
who is trying to <outcome>
and currently uses <alternative>,
the venture enables <differentiated outcome>
through <mechanism>.
<payer> pays for <unit of value>
and the initial customer is reached through <channel>.
The current stage is <stage>, and the decisive uncertainty is <unknown>.
```

### Value path

```text
TRIGGER -> DISCOVERY -> DECISION -> ADOPTION -> FIRST VALUE -> REPEAT VALUE -> PAYMENT -> SUPPORT OR RENEWAL
```

Name the actor, friction, required proof, and venture responsibility at each step.

### Model checks

- Is the beachhead internally consistent rather than an average of segments?
- Is the buyer's value connected to the user's outcome?
- Can the team reach the first credible customers without assuming an unbuilt channel?
- Does the full service, including onboarding and support, deliver the promise?
- Does value capture align with when and how value is created?
- Does the model build a compounding asset, or only a one-off project?
- Which expansion condition must be true before adding a segment, product, or geography?

## 6. Assumptions and Experiments

### Assumption map

Classify:

- **Desirability:** customer problem, urgency, adoption, switching, willingness to pay.
- **Viability:** pricing, revenue quality, contribution, acquisition, retention, cash and capital.
- **Feasibility:** technical, delivery, operational, quality, privacy, safety, regulatory.
- **Adaptability:** timing, policy, platform, ecosystem, incumbent response.
- **People:** access, capacity, capability, incentives, governance.

Prioritize assumptions by consequence, uncertainty, and time to an irreversible commitment.

### Experiment card

```markdown
Decision:
Assumption:
Current evidence:
Contradiction:
Method:
Target participant or unit:
Observable measure:
Support threshold:
Pivot threshold:
Stop threshold:
Guardrails:
Known confounds:
Owner:
Timebox:
Cost ceiling:
Decision changed by result:
```

Set thresholds before seeing results. Choose the strongest feasible evidence within the ethical, legal, time, and resource constraints.

### Evidence cautions

- Interviews can explain problems and mechanisms; they do not prove payment or retention.
- A signed letter of intent shows more commitment than praise but less than collected revenue.
- A paid pilot shows initial commitment but may still depend on bespoke delivery.
- Revenue does not establish positive contribution or repeatable acquisition.
- Repeated retained use does not prove a large reachable market.
- Failed experiments can reduce risk when the result is traceable and the decision changes.

## 7. Economic Coherence

Use period-consistent, sourced inputs or variables:

```text
Revenue per unit = price x paid units x collection rate
Contribution per unit = revenue per unit - direct variable delivery cost
Contribution margin = contribution per unit / revenue per unit
Break-even units = fixed period cost / contribution per unit
Acquisition payback = acquisition cost / contribution generated per period
Runway = available cash / net cash burn per period
```

Adjust the model for marketplaces, usage pricing, services, inventory, credit risk, or long collection cycles. Include onboarding, human delivery, support, refunds, transaction fees, channel share, and working-capital effects when material.

At early stage:

- show low, base, and high scenarios;
- identify the two or three variables that change the decision;
- distinguish booked, billed, collected, recognized, and recurring revenue where relevant;
- avoid valuation precision without a clear purpose and defensible method;
- route detailed accounting, tax, financing, and control questions to specialists.

## 8. Decision Gates

### Build

Use only when:

- the responsible MVP tests the decisive uncertainty;
- material trust, safety, privacy, legal, and delivery conditions are bounded;
- cost and time are capped;
- success and stop thresholds are explicit;
- the owner accepts the remaining assumptions.

### Test

Use when a cheaper, faster, or safer experiment can change the commitment decision.

### Pivot

Use when evidence supports an underlying problem or asset but contradicts the current segment, value proposition, solution mechanism, channel, pricing, or model. State what remains invariant and what changes.

### Pause

Use when a dependency, timing condition, evidence-access problem, or capacity constraint blocks responsible progress without disproving the venture. Define a resume condition and review date.

### Kill

Use when:

- a core problem or buying assumption is contradicted;
- economics remain structurally unattractive under credible sensitivity;
- the route to customers is inaccessible;
- the downside or responsibility risk is unacceptable;
- the team cannot close a critical capability or incentive gap;
- more research is unlikely to change the decision at reasonable cost.

Do not use `Pause` to avoid an evidence-backed `Kill`.

## 9. Specialist Handoffs

Prepare a bounded handoff:

- **Customer discovery:** assumptions, target roles, current evidence, research questions, thresholds, and privacy constraints.
- **Engineering:** business experiment, customer outcome, scope, quality and risk guardrails, cost ceiling, and decision rule.
- **Startup finance:** sourced inputs, unit-economics variables, cash timing, scenarios, financing dependency, and missing controls.
- **Legal, tax, privacy, safety, or regulatory:** exact question, jurisdiction, business facts, commitment date, and consequence of uncertainty.
- **CEO plan review:** source-of-truth plan, accepted venture thesis, unresolved strategic choices, commitment requested, and evidence ledger.

Do not imply that a handoff is an approval.
