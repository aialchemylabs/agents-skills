# Build Decision-Ready B2B Proposals

Turn a customer opportunity into a defensible pursuit decision and, when warranted, a compliant, persuasive, deliverable, and commercially sound proposal. Optimize for a good customer outcome and a good contract, not for document completion alone.

## Preserve Authority and Evidence

- Treat every proposal as a potential commitment. Do not submit, send, sign, accept terms, contact the buyer, or represent an approval without explicit authorization.
- Identify the source of truth and record document names, dates, versions, amendments, and buyer clarifications.
- Separate verified evidence, user-supplied facts, stakeholder claims, estimates, assumptions, unknowns, contradictions, and recommendations.
- Do not invent customer requirements, demand, budgets, evaluation criteria, results, credentials, references, certifications, delivery capacity, prices, competitor behavior, or approval status.
- Mark unresolved release-critical content as `[CONFIRM: owner and question]`. Label hypothetical structures and values `Illustrative`.
- Keep confidential solicitation and customer material inside the authorized context.
- Browse and cite current primary or authoritative sources when a material requirement depends on current procurement rules, regulation, standards, or public facts.
- Route legal, tax, accounting, privacy, security, insurance, employment, and regulatory conclusions to the accountable specialist. State the exact question and status; do not imply clearance.

## Select the Smallest Working Mode

Choose one or combine only what the request requires:

1. **Qualify** — recommend `bid`, `shape/clarify`, `partner`, `pilot`, `reprice`, or `no-bid`.
2. **Plan** — build proposal controls, buyer/evaluator mapping, win strategy, offer structure, owners, and review calendar.
3. **Draft** — write grounded proposal, tender-response, or statement-of-work content from accepted evidence and scope.
4. **Price** — test buyer value, seller contribution, cash exposure, packages, and payment structure.
5. **Red-team** — independently test compliance, scoring strength, proof, deliverability, economics, risk, and readiness.

State the mode and excluded work. Do not force a complete proposal onto an opportunity that should first be clarified, piloted, repriced, partnered, or declined.

## Load the Operating Playbook

Read [references/proposal-playbook.md](references/proposal-playbook.md) before substantial work. Use its evidence ledger, hard gates, compliance matrix, commercial models, delivery controls, readiness template, and source notes. Scale the artifacts to the decision.

## Establish the Pursuit Brief

Resolve:

- buyer organization, proposal type, source package, jurisdiction, deadline, time zone, and submission mechanism;
- customer problem, desired outcome, baseline, urgency, cost of inaction, current alternative, and adoption burden;
- beneficiary, users, champion, economic buyer, evaluator, procurement, technical and security reviewers, legal, finance, implementers, approver, and blockers;
- evaluation criteria and weights, mandatory conditions, budget evidence, competitors or substitutes, incumbent, and relationship state;
- seller objective, delivery capacity, relevant proof, pursuit cost, commercial limits, accountable owners, and next decision date.

Ask one concise blocking question only when the answer changes eligibility, mandatory compliance, feasibility, price authority, or the requested artifact. Otherwise proceed with visibly labeled uncertainty and a clarification plan.

## Build the Evidence Ledger Before Claims

Record every material fact or commitment with its type, source, version or date, owner, confidence, decision impact, and allowed treatment. Preserve speaker and context for buyer or internal claims.

Prefer executed contracts, paid use, measured outcomes, issued solicitation documents, formal buyer clarifications, approved internal records, and named decision-maker evidence over analogy or intuition. Lower-quality evidence may justify reversible discovery; it does not justify an irreversible fixed commitment.

## Qualify Before Spending or Drafting

Evaluate the hard gates:

- scope fit and eligibility;
- mandatory compliance;
- real buyer need and decision path;
- solution feasibility and required proof;
- delivery capacity and timeline;
- deal economics, cash timing, and opportunity cost;
- commercial, security, privacy, ethical, and reputational risk;
- accountable authority for price, claims, terms, and submission.

Treat a material `fail` as incompatible with a clean `bid`. Treat a material `unknown` as a reason to clarify, pilot, partner, reprice, or decline—not as a neutral score.

Then assess customer value, decision access, win plausibility, strategic fit, differentiation, seller downside, capacity displacement, delivery risk, and evidence quality. State decisive evidence, confidence, accepted trade-off, stop conditions, owner, and next gate. Do not manufacture win probability or average away a hard failure.

## Control Formal Requests

Read the complete request package before drafting. Preserve the buyer's numbering and required order.

- Build a document-control snapshot for deadlines, time zones, formats, page or word limits, forms, signatures, attachments, questions, departures, and submission rules.
- Build a requirement-to-response matrix for every instruction, mandatory condition, scored criterion, deliverable, term, declaration, and administrative requirement.
- Track amendments and Q&A. Escalate conflicting instructions instead of choosing silently.
- Mark status `compliant`, `partial`, `noncompliant`, `unknown`, or `not applicable` with evidence. Planned content is not compliance.
- Put material ambiguity in a clarification log with its decision impact, proposed question, owner, deadline, and fallback. Do not contact the issuer without authorization.
- Treat unresolved mandatory items as readiness blockers.

## Design the Evaluator-Aligned Offer

Map each material criterion to:

```text
buyer outcome
+ relevant and defensible discriminator
+ supportable proof
+ decision significance
+ delivery risk control
```

Limit principal win themes to three. Reject generic superiority, unsupported competitor claims, and themes any rival could reuse unchanged.

Define:

- measurable outcomes, deliverables, boundaries, exclusions, and non-goals;
- buyer and seller responsibilities, assumptions, dependencies, milestones, governance, and escalation;
- objective acceptance criteria, evidence, remedies, support, adoption, warranty, transition, and exit;
- data, security, privacy, IP, licensing, third-party, and specialist-review dependencies;
- change initiation, impact assessment, authorization, price and schedule adjustment, and audit trail.

Use discovery, a paid pilot, phases, capped effort, or a partner when uncertainty makes a broad fixed promise irresponsible. Do not hide buyer implementation work or absorb unbounded customization.

## Test Value, Economics, Price, and Cash

Keep internal economics separate from customer-facing price presentation.

- Model buyer baseline, realized benefit, total cost of ownership, timing, adoption, and alternatives only from defensible inputs.
- Model net revenue, direct and variable delivery cost, third parties, support, warranty, rework, contingency, contribution, approved overhead treatment, taxes, payment timing, and maximum cash trough.
- Reconcile units, currency, scope, assumptions, acceptance, invoicing, discounts, and totals.
- Use downside, base, and upside scenarios where uncertainty could change the decision. Show formulas and sources.
- Compare the offer with approved contribution, cash, capacity, risk, and opportunity-cost limits.
- Recommend one commercial structure and explain its risk allocation. Offer no more than three coherent options when different terms reveal real buyer preferences.
- Exchange discounts or concessions for valuable commitments. Do not invent willingness to pay, competitor price, margin approval, or discount authority.

Flag fixed scope with unstable acceptance, margin that depends on optimistic staffing, payment tied to buyer-controlled events, unaffordable working capital, uncapped obligations, and risk transferred to the party least able to control it.

## Draft From Evidence, Not Aspiration

Follow the solicitation's order. Otherwise use only the sections required to answer the buyer's decision:

1. outcome and recommendation;
2. current state, urgency, and success criteria;
3. proposed approach and why it fits;
4. scope, responsibilities, acceptance, and change control;
5. implementation, adoption, governance, team, support, and transition;
6. relevant proof and risk reduction;
7. buyer value and measurement;
8. pricing, payment, validity, and options;
9. assumptions, dependencies, exclusions, departures, and material terms;
10. next decision and mutual action plan.

Answer the requirement first. Put proof beside the claim. Label estimates and proposed commitments. Remove brochure copy, unsupported adjectives, hidden exceptions, duplicated content, and internal cost, margin, or negotiation notes not approved for disclosure.

## Red-Team the Commitment

Run independent passes for:

1. compliance and submission;
2. evaluator and buyer decision;
3. solution, delivery, adoption, and transition;
4. evidence and claim integrity;
5. price, contribution, cash, tax basis, and opportunity cost;
6. contract, data, security, privacy, and operational risk;
7. executive and specialist approvals;
8. document control and production.

Classify findings as `blocking`, `conditional`, or `advisory`. Return `ready`, `ready with conditions`, `not ready`, or `no-bid recommended`. Never return `ready` with unresolved mandatory compliance, unsupported material claims, unapproved economics, rejected delivery feasibility, or required specialist review still pending.

## Produce the Negotiation Handoff, Not Negotiation Coaching

When proposal work is ready to enter negotiation, hand off:

- final submitted or proposed package and document version;
- approved target and reservation package, pricing and term authority, and walk-away triggers;
- deal-specific contribution, cash, delivery, precedent, and risk effect of material changes;
- open contractual issues, buyer requests, assumptions, dependencies, and unresolved evidence;
- approval owners, delegation limits, required specialists, and expiry dates;
- facts safe to disclose and internal information that must remain confidential.

Label unverified buyer interests or limits as hypotheses. Route interest mapping, BATNA strategy, ZOPA, anchors, concession sequencing, conversation scripts, difficult behavior, and live negotiation coaching to `prepare-founder-negotiations`.

## Close the Loop

- Do not submit or contact the buyer; prepare the final control checklist and name the authorized human action.
- After an authorized submission, preserve the submitted version and receipt evidence when supplied.
- For clarifications or best-and-final revisions, revalidate compliance, economics, scope, risk, and authority.
- On award, reconcile the proposal, negotiated terms, final contract, assumptions, and delivery handoff before work starts.
- On loss, cancellation, or withdrawal, use permitted buyer feedback for a debrief. Separate documented feedback from speculation and retain no restricted material beyond authorization.

End with the smallest action that materially improves the pursuit decision or advances the approved response.
