# Prepare Founder Negotiations

Prepare a deliberate, ethical negotiation that improves the quality of the decision, the agreement, and the working relationship. Seek an implementable outcome better than the credible alternative—not agreement at any cost.

## Preserve Authority, Truth, and Safety

- Distinguish facts, user-supplied statements, counterpart claims, estimates, assumptions, hypotheses, unknowns, and recommendations.
- Do not invent authority, competing offers, deadlines, scarcity, market benchmarks, BATNAs, reservation points, or the counterpart's private interests.
- Never recommend deception, false urgency, fake alternatives, hidden side deals, coercion, unlawful discrimination, retaliation, bribery, or misuse of confidential information.
- Treat the counterpart as a decision maker with legitimate interests, not a target to manipulate. Separate the people and relationship from the substantive problem.
- Do not send messages, make offers, accept terms, disclose confidential limits, sign, or bind any party without explicit authorization.
- Route legal, securities, tax, accounting, employment, governance, IP, competition, privacy, and regulatory questions to qualified owners. State the question and status; do not imply clearance.
- Browse and cite current primary or authoritative sources when objective criteria or legal and market context materially affect the recommendation.

## Load the Negotiation Playbook

Read [references/negotiation-playbook.md](references/negotiation-playbook.md) before substantial preparation. Use its issue map, BATNA test, option design, conversation plan, implementation check, domain prompts, and source notes.

## Confirm the Mandate

Establish:

- parties, constituencies, decision makers, influencers, approvers, and authorized negotiator;
- decision, issues, history, deadline, process, venue, sequence, and communication channel;
- stakes, relationship horizon, power dependencies, confidentiality, and reputational concerns;
- already-made commitments, non-negotiable obligations, delegation limits, and required approvals;
- the user's desired outcome, credible alternatives, risks, and what a responsible no-agreement outcome looks like.

State whether the work is preparation, message drafting, offer review, role-play, or debrief. Ask one concise blocking question only when the missing answer would change authority, legality, the reservation boundary, or the requested action. Otherwise continue with labeled uncertainty.

For a customer proposal negotiation, require the proposal handoff: current package and version, approved economics and limits, scope and delivery effects, contractual issues, confidentiality boundary, and approval owners. If absent, prepare only the discovery questions and explicitly block numeric or contractual commitments.

## Frame the Negotiation System

Map:

- each party's stated positions;
- underlying economic, operational, strategic, relational, identity, fairness, timing, and risk interests;
- internal constituents who must approve or implement the agreement;
- linked negotiations, coalitions, precedent effects, and future rounds;
- decision process, agenda, authority, sequencing, and information asymmetry.

Attribute known interests and label inferred interests as hypotheses to test. Do not diagnose motives as facts. In a difficult conversation, acknowledge emotion and impact without conceding an unsupported account.

## Build and Test Alternatives

Define the user's BATNA as a concrete course of action if no agreement is reached.

- Verify that the alternative is available, lawful, fundable, and executable.
- Estimate timing, cost, risk, reversibility, relationship impact, and implementation owner.
- Improve the alternative before increasing pressure where practical.
- Estimate the counterpart's alternatives only from observable evidence and label them hypotheses.
- Ignore sunk negotiation costs when comparing the proposed agreement with the BATNA.

Derive:

- a target or aspiration package;
- a reservation package representing indifference between agreement and BATNA;
- approval limits and walk-away triggers;
- any estimated zone of possible agreement.

Do not claim a ZOPA when the counterpart's reservation boundary is unknown. State instead what information would test whether one exists. Treat the reservation point as a package across issues, not automatically as one price.

## Separate Interests From Positions

For each issue:

1. record each stated position;
2. ask what interest, fear, constraint, or success measure may sit beneath it;
3. identify compatible, conflicting, and differently valued interests;
4. identify objective criteria both parties could accept as legitimate;
5. define what evidence would change the recommendation.

Use questions and active listening to test hypotheses. Do not reveal the user's BATNA, reservation point, internal urgency, or confidential trade priorities unless disclosure is deliberately authorized and advantageous.

## Create Value Before Dividing It

Expand beyond a single headline issue. Consider:

- timing, milestones, term, governance, information rights, vesting, decision rights, role clarity, scope, support, payment, risk, exclusivity, references, performance conditions, exit, and review points;
- trades across issues valued differently by the parties;
- contingent terms when parties disagree about the future and outcomes can be measured, attributed, audited, and bounded;
- no more than three multiple equivalent simultaneous offers when they can reveal preferences without hiding material differences.

Make each package internally coherent, implementable, and at least as good as the reservation package. Do not use fake choices, unbounded earn-outs, vague future promises, or terms that transfer risk to a party unable to control it.

## Use Objective Criteria Carefully

Select standards for independence, relevance, comparability, recency, geography, role, stage, and methodology. Prefer mutually accepted market data, precedent, professional standards, audited measures, cost estimates, legal norms confirmed by counsel, or neutral expert input.

Do not cherry-pick a favorable benchmark or treat a median as an entitlement. When standards conflict, compare their applicability and agree on the selection method before arguing over the result.

## Plan the Conversation

Prepare:

1. opening purpose and relationship signal;
2. agenda and decision process;
3. diagnostic questions and listening plan;
4. information to share, protect, or disclose conditionally;
5. objective criteria and evidence;
6. target package and rationale;
7. likely objections, emotion, silence, pressure, or impasse;
8. planned trades, concession sequence, and give-get rules;
9. authority checks, caucus triggers, and pause language;
10. close, written recap, approval, and implementation steps.

Anchor only when there is a defensible basis and sufficient information. Explain the rationale. Never anchor with a knowingly false fact.

Make every concession conditional and reciprocal:

```text
If you can commit to X, I can seek approval for Y.
```

Reduce concessions deliberately; do not bid against yourself. When pushed beyond authority or the reservation package, pause, test the underlying interest, offer an authorized trade, or walk away. Do not improvise approval.

## Adapt Without Losing the Core Method

- **Cofounder** — address roles, decision rights, effort, vesting, IP, pay, capital, information, deadlock, departures, and relationship repair. Do not reduce the negotiation to an equity percentage.
- **Investor** — address capital, dilution, economics, control, information, governance, future financing, founder obligations, signaling, and downside. When the mandate lacks a reconciled cash forecast, financing need, or runway evidence, obtain the approved finance handoff from `startup-finance-and-controls` rather than validating those inputs inside the negotiation. Require a current cap table and instrument scenarios from the accountable finance or legal owner, plus securities and corporate counsel.
- **Hiring** — address role, outcomes, authority, base, variable pay, equity, vesting, benefits, flexibility, review, and exit while respecting employment law and internal equity.
- **Vendor or partner** — address scope, service, volume, payment, IP, data, exclusivity, governance, change, continuity, remedies, and exit.
- **Difficult stakeholder or dispute** — address impact, facts, interpretations, interests, repair, decision rights, escalation, and future working rules. Use a neutral facilitator when safety, power imbalance, or conflict severity warrants it.

## Test Agreement Quality and Implementation

Before recommending acceptance, compare the whole package with the BATNA and reservation package. Test:

- value distribution and whether critical interests are met;
- authority, specialist approval, and consistency with prior commitments;
- incentives, dependencies, milestones, measurement, and accountability;
- ambiguity, asymmetry, unintended precedent, and downside scenarios;
- governance, change, dispute, review, exit, and enforcement paths;
- who communicates, documents, approves, implements, and verifies each obligation.

Distinguish `proposed`, `agreed in principle`, `approved`, `signed`, and `implemented`. A verbal understanding, email recap, or term sheet may have legal effects; require counsel where material and do not characterize it as binding or non-binding without verified advice.

## Deliver a Decision-Ready Brief

Return only the sections needed:

1. mandate, authority, and negotiation context;
2. facts, claims, assumptions, unknowns, and evidence gaps;
3. parties, constituencies, positions, and interests;
4. BATNA quality, target, reservation package, and conditional ZOPA assessment;
5. prioritized issue map and objective criteria;
6. value-creation options and coherent packages;
7. conversation, disclosure, anchor, concession, and impasse plan;
8. approval, legal or specialist, documentation, and implementation gates;
9. recommended next move, owner, stop conditions, and debrief questions.

For message drafting or role-play, preserve the same boundaries. Do not convert hypothetical figures or unsourced claims into confident spoken language.

End with a clear recommendation: proceed, gather information, improve the BATNA, seek approval, use a facilitator, pause, or walk away.
