## 1. Evidence Standards

Build an evidence ledger before making a high-confidence recommendation.

| Type | Meaning | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Fact | Directly supported by supplied or inspected evidence | Cite or anchor it |
| Claim | Stated by a stakeholder but not independently verified | Attribute it |
| Assumption | Belief required for the plan to work | Make it testable |
| Unknown | Material missing information | Name its decision impact |
| Inference | Conclusion drawn from facts or claims | Explain the reasoning |
| Contradiction | Evidence points in different directions | Do not average it away |

Use an evidence ladder:

1. observed behavior, paid use, retention, or repeated operational evidence;
2. pilots, signed commitments, credible experiments, or comparable internal data;
3. interviews about recent behavior and current alternatives;
4. surveys, stated preference, expert judgment, and market analogies;
5. founder intuition and untested narrative.

Lower-rung evidence can justify a reversible experiment. It rarely justifies an irreversible commitment by itself.

For current market, competitor, pricing, regulation, or economic claims:

- prefer primary and authoritative sources;
- record the date and geography;
- separate a market-wide fact from its relevance to this company;
- mark scenario estimates as estimates;
- never turn search-result volume into demand evidence.

## 2. Review Postures

| Posture | Core question | Scope treatment | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope expansion | What substantially better outcome becomes possible? | Propose ambitious additions individually | Greenfield bets, explicit “think bigger” requests |
| Selective expansion | Is the baseline sound, and which optional gains are worth adding? | Preserve baseline; keep additions opt-in | Product iterations and credible plans with upside |
| Hold scope | Is this exact commitment decision-ready? | No additions or cuts | Fixed commitments, urgent decisions, explicit constraints |
| Scope reduction | What is the smallest path that proves or delivers the core value? | Cut audiences, channels, features, and commitments deliberately | Overbuilt plans, weak evidence, tight runway |

Posture is not a quality score. Expansion is not automatically visionary, and reduction is not automatically disciplined.

### Scope expansion

- Describe the felt customer or stakeholder outcome before proposing mechanisms.
- Ask what creates an order-of-magnitude gain for a plausible increase in effort or risk.
- Separate a true strategic unlock from a pile of adjacent features.
- Present each expansion as accept, defer, or reject.
- State new assumptions and operational load created by every accepted expansion.

### Selective expansion

- Review the baseline first.
- Surface only the highest-leverage optional additions.
- Keep the recommendation neutral unless one option clearly dominates.
- Do not make optional infrastructure or polish a hidden prerequisite.

### Hold scope

- Do not relitigate ambition.
- Test whether the business case, adoption path, economics, capacity, metrics, and risk controls support the stated commitment.
- Surface contradictions without converting them into unsolicited scope changes.

### Scope reduction

- Preserve the customer outcome, not the proposed feature list.
- Prefer one segment, one painful use case, one adoption path, and one decisive learning goal.
- Separate “must ship together” from “nice to ship together.”
- Protect essential trust, compliance, quality, and measurement work.

## 3. Business Review Passes

### Pass 1: Customer and problem evidence

Test:

- the target segment, user, buyer, decision maker, and beneficiary;
- the job, trigger, current alternative, frequency, urgency, and cost of inaction;
- whether evidence describes recent behavior rather than hypothetical intent;
- whether the plan confuses a vocal customer, internal stakeholder, or competitor move with a market;
- switching barriers, trust requirements, and reasons to do nothing;
- the riskiest customer assumption and the cheapest decisive test.

Warning signs:

- “everyone” is the target customer;
- praise is counted as validation;
- the solution appears in the problem statement;
- no current alternative is named;
- urgency depends only on an internal deadline.

### Pass 2: Product value and experience

Test:

- the before-and-after customer state;
- the smallest moment where value becomes obvious;
- the complete journey from trigger through adoption, value, recovery, and repeat use;
- whether the value proposition is specific enough to choose over the status quo;
- whether trust, support, service delivery, and failure recovery are part of the promise;
- whether expansion ideas improve the outcome or merely increase surface area.

Use this compact arc when useful:

```text
TRIGGER -> CURRENT FRICTION -> FIRST VALUE -> REPEAT VALUE -> RETENTION OR EXPANSION
```

### Pass 3: Strategy, timing, and differentiation

Test:

- strategic fit with mission, positioning, existing strengths, and explicit priorities;
- why now: customer, technology, regulation, channel, cost, or organizational change;
- differentiation against direct competitors, substitutes, internal workarounds, and inaction;
- whether the advantage is durable, compounding, or quickly copied;
- what the company will not do as a consequence of this choice;
- whether the plan builds a capability or distracts from the core.

Do not accept “AI-powered,” “better UX,” “first mover,” or “all-in-one” as differentiation without evidence.

### Pass 4: Business model and economics

Test:

- who pays, for what, how much, how often, and from which budget;
- value creation, delivery, and capture;
- gross margin, delivery load, support burden, working capital, cash timing, and founder attention;
- acquisition, onboarding, retention, expansion, and failure costs;
- pricing logic and willingness-to-pay evidence;
- sensitivity to the two or three assumptions that most affect viability;
- capital, partner, hiring, or contractual commitments.

When numbers are missing, define the variables and break-even relationship. Do not invent values.

### Pass 5: Go-to-market, adoption, and retention

Test:

- the beachhead segment and use case;
- route to the first ten credible customers or users;
- channel ownership, sales motion, cycle length, implementation burden, and buyer proof;
- activation, behavior change, migration, training, trust, and procurement barriers;
- retention mechanism and expansion path;
- channel conflict, partner dependence, concentration, and repeatability;
- whether the plan contains a distribution strategy or merely a launch event.

### Pass 6: Execution capacity and operating model

Test:

- accountable owner and decision rights;
- skills, time, capital, partners, data, and leadership attention required;
- what current work is paused or underfunded;
- dependencies, sequencing, and handoffs;
- service delivery, support, quality control, and escalation;
- wartime versus peacetime posture and whether the cadence fits;
- the first 30 days, next decision checkpoint, and kill criteria.

Plans compete for capacity even when software implementation is fast.

### Pass 7: Risks, stakeholders, ethics, and reversibility

Classify material choices:

| Choice | Magnitude | Reversibility | Stakeholders exposed | Evidence needed before commitment |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Test:

- customer, employee, partner, investor, community, and regulatory consequences;
- reputation, privacy, safety, fairness, misuse, and incentive risks;
- legal, accounting, tax, employment, financing, and sector-specific review needs;
- concentration, platform, vendor, and key-person dependence;
- downside containment, stop conditions, and recovery;
- whether success creates a new risk the plan ignores.

Recommend qualified professional review where required. Do not imply professional clearance.

### Pass 8: Metrics, experiments, and decision gates

Build a measurement chain:

```text
ASSUMPTION -> EXPERIMENT -> LEADING SIGNAL -> OUTCOME -> GUARDRAIL -> DECISION RULE
```

Test:

- one primary outcome and the smallest set of explanatory drivers;
- metric definition, source, grain, window, exclusions, baseline, and target;
- leading indicators that arrive before the commitment becomes expensive;
- guardrails for quality, trust, economics, or stakeholder harm;
- pass, pivot, pause, and stop thresholds;
- owner and review date;
- whether measurement can distinguish adoption failure from product-value failure.

Avoid vanity metrics and unsupported causal stories.

### Pass 9: Long-term trajectory and option value

Map:

```text
CURRENT STATE -> THIS COMMITMENT -> 12-MONTH CAPABILITY -> OPTIONS CREATED OR CLOSED
```

Test:

- path dependence and one-way doors;
- compounding assets such as distribution, data, trust, brand, workflow position, or capability;
- organizational debt, operational debt, and strategic distraction;
- what becomes easier or harder next;
- whether the plan remains coherent if it succeeds at 10x expected demand;
- whether a reversible experiment preserves more option value than a full commitment.

## 4. Decision and Finding Formats

### Material decision

```markdown
Decision: <one choice>
Why it matters: <stake and deadline>
Evidence: <facts, claims, assumptions, and unknowns>
Recommendation: <one option> because <decisive reason>
Door type: <one-way or two-way>

A. <option> — <outcome, cost, risk, reversibility>
B. <option> — <outcome, cost, risk, reversibility>
C. <option, only if genuinely distinct> — <outcome, cost, risk, reversibility>
```

### Finding

```markdown
[P1|P2|P3] <concise business issue>
Evidence: <source or explicit gap>
Consequence: <decision or operating impact>
Recommendation: <smallest complete response>
Confidence: <high, medium, or low and why>
Decision needed by: <date or milestone, if known>
```

Priority guide:

- `P1` — invalidates the premise, risks material harm, or makes the commitment irresponsible;
- `P2` — materially weakens adoption, economics, execution, or learning and should be resolved;
- `P3` — useful improvement, option, or follow-up that need not block the decision.

Use an artifact only when it clarifies relationships.

### Alternative table

## 6. Final Report Template

```markdown
# CEO Plan Review

## Verdict
<Ready | Ready with decisions | Not ready | Discovery required> — <reason>

## CEO recommendation
<one path, why, and accepted tradeoff>

## Decision and evidence snapshot
<owner, customer, outcome, deadline, facts, assumptions, unknowns>

## Premise challenge
<problem, proxy risk, do-nothing case, why now>

## Review posture and scope
<posture, baseline, unapproved or accepted additions, deferrals, cuts>

## Alternatives
<comparison table and recommendation>

## Findings by business pass
<prioritized evidence-backed findings; no material issue where appropriate>

## One-way doors and material risks
<risk register or concise list>

## Metrics and learning plan
<assumptions, experiments, metrics, guardrails, thresholds, review date>

## Next actions
<smallest decision-improving actions>

## Unresolved decisions
<priority order; distinguish recommendations from accepted choices>

## Specialist handoffs
<only the reviews actually needed>
```

## 7. Specialist Handoffs

Recommend a handoff only when it is material:

- customer discovery for weak problem or demand evidence;
- market research or sizing for a decision that depends on current external facts;
- finance or accounting for formal reporting, tax, controls, or material economic commitments;
- legal or compliance for contracts, employment, privacy, safety, financing, securities, or regulation;
- engineering plan review after business scope and outcomes are accepted;
- design-plan review for material user journeys and interaction decisions;
- KPI reporting when the decision moves from planning into an operating cadence.

State what question the specialist must answer. Do not use a handoff to avoid making the CEO-level recommendation.
