# Outreach Playbook

Read the sections needed for the current task. The examples are adaptable patterns, not mandatory wording or promises about results.

## 1. Openings and Value

Build the opening from: honest identity and purpose → verified relevance → a credible outcome → a small invitation to respond. For a cold call, seek permission before a longer pitch and pause for the answer. Use a warm, direct tone without pretending to be an old acquaintance.

### Direct customer

Message pattern:

> Hi [name], I noticed [verified business context]. At [company], we help [relevant teams] address [specific workflow] through [credible approach]. Is [problem or outcome] a priority for your team at the moment?

Call pattern:

> Hi [name], this is [sender] from [company]. I'm calling about [relevant workflow]. May I take a few seconds to explain why I thought it might be relevant?

If they agree, give one useful sentence about the outcome, then ask about their current situation. Do not squeeze the entire qualification process into the opening.

### Sales partner

> Hi [name], your work in [verified area] caught my attention. We deliver [scoped service]. I'm exploring a partnership where you lead sales and the client relationship while we handle implementation. Is there a client need where delivery capacity would help you win the work?

Test their actual ownership of sales, not just access to a network. An introduction-only arrangement can be useful, but label it as such. Do not imply commission-only, exclusivity or an agreed margin unless supported.

### Paid sales provider

> Hi [name], I saw that you offer [verified service]. We deliver [offer] and are looking for someone to prospect, qualify and lead the sale. Do you take hands-on responsibility for selling services like ours?

Qualify scope and cost before treating a provider as a viable partner. A meeting-setting service is not necessarily a closing service.

### Value discipline

Explain how the offer could improve an identified problem. Use customer-supported baselines, feasible scope and approved evidence before quantifying benefits. State assumptions in any estimate; don't translate a prospect's title into a supposed pain point or claim savings from unrelated work. Value exceeding price is a useful framing device, not proof of willingness to buy or effortless closing.

Start with one main question. Ask for a meeting after interest or a clear reason to meet; don't mechanically end every sentence with a question. Different channels and levels of familiarity need different phrasing.

## 2. Qualification by Commercial Route

Start from a concrete recent example: the last time the workflow caused trouble, a similar service was sold, or an outsourced sales engagement was run. Listen before asking the next relevant question.

| Dimension | Customer | Sales partner | Paid sales provider |
|---|---|---|---|
| Need | What happens today, what has been tried, and what would need to improve? | Which client problem can they sell a solution to, and why do they need external delivery? | What experience and execution capacity match this offer and target buyer? |
| Authority | Who uses, sponsors, approves, pays and can block the decision? | Who approves the partnership, and who owns each stage through closing? | Who will actually run the account, and what selling remains with the user? |
| Budget / economics | What funding path exists and how would a paid first phase be approved? | Who funds delivery, and how might fees, margin and payment timing work? | What are upfront fees, retainers, minimum terms, variable fees and pilot options? |
| Timing | What event drives the next decision, and when could a paid phase start? | Is there a current client opportunity and a dated next action? | When can the assigned operator start and when will progress be reviewed? |

Use these prompts naturally, not as an interrogation. Budget undisclosed is **unknown**, not zero. No current allocation may warrant learning the approval process. Confirmed lack of a feasible funding path can justify pausing. Do not pressure people to find money or infer personal finances.

A credible near-term opportunity needs evidence of a relevant problem, an owner and a concrete next action. Record qualification gaps honestly. A partner can be relevant even when the end client, rather than the partner, would pay for delivery.

## 3. Objections and Next Steps

Use **acknowledge → diagnose → clarify the remaining concern → respond**. One objection may contain several issues; do not pressure the prospect to declare it the only obstacle.

| Response | Useful diagnostic direction | Possible next step |
|---|---|---|
| Too expensive | Compare scope, alternatives, implementation effort, margin or payment timing. | Examine a smaller viable paid phase within approved terms. |
| No budget | Distinguish unavailable funding, unallocated funds, uncertain value and the wrong budget owner. | Pause, identify a real approval milestone, or agree a later check-in. |
| We already have a provider | Understand what works and whether any relevant gap exists. | Discuss only a supported complementary need, or accept no fit. |
| Not sure | Ask what information would help them decide whether a first step is worthwhile. | Supply the missing evidence or agree that the case is insufficient. |
| Too busy | Check whether there is interest at another time without treating delay as a commitment. | Offer a short note or later conversation if welcome. |

If a 1–10 fit rating helps a rehearsal or live conversation, explore both what supports the rating and what is missing. It is not a technique to force agreement. Do not invent discounts, guarantees, references or calendar availability to overcome an objection.

For a meeting, summarise the reason and intended outcome, then offer genuine available times with timezone or ask for availability. Use visible placeholders when availability is unknown. Confirm the agreed contact route; never claim an invite or reminder exists until it does.

## 4. Follow-up and Contact Questions

**Meeting declined, but still engaged:** ask whether a brief note or later check-in would be useful. If there is no fit but the person remains open, ask whether they know a more relevant commercial contact. These are alternatives guided by the response, not a compulsory three-step sequence.

**No response:** silence is not consent or interest. If the user requests a sequence, propose a bounded cadence appropriate to the relationship and channel, with each message adding useful context and an end condition. Don't send it automatically or persist across channels to evade a refusal.

**Explicit stop:** acknowledge and end. Do not attach another pitch, nurture offer or referral request. Record the restriction in the output; change external records only within the user's authorised scope.

**Where did you get my number?** identify the actual number source if known. Profile discovery on LinkedIn does not mean the number came from LinkedIn. If the source cannot be verified, say so; if the person wants the conversation to end, stop. Do not promise removal from every system unless that action can and will be completed.

## 5. Rehearsal and Review

For interactive rehearsal, identify the scenario and play the prospect one response at a time. Base the role on supplied professional context and label invented objections or details as simulation. Keep answers realistic; do not immediately reward any script with a meeting. Debrief when requested or at the natural end.

Review against:

- relevance and factual support;
- clarity of identity, purpose and value;
- listening and questions that uncover the actual situation;
- appropriate qualification for the commercial route;
- honest, proportionate objection handling;
- a mutually understood next step or respectful stop.

Use quotes or timestamps from supplied material to support feedback. Separate what was observed from interpretation. A text script permits comments on wording and pauses written into it, not claims about the speaker's actual voice. Audio permits supported feedback on pace, interruption and delivery without inferring personal traits.

For campaign review, compare like-for-like cohorts and define each outcome. State the observation window, numerator and denominator, missing delivery information and selection effects. Recommend one meaningful change to test and the evidence that would justify keeping it; do not promise a particular lift.

## 6. Research Basis and Limits

This playbook adapts concepts from a user-supplied **Saad Khaja cold-calling masterclass cheatsheet**: brief openings, value framing, qualification, objection diagnosis and next-step options. The full original recording was not independently verified. The scripts here are original adaptations, not a verbatim course transcript or an endorsement. Fixed timing, question-control and effortless-closing claims are treated as heuristics, not established laws.

Sources reviewed on **2026-09-16**:

- [Gong: cold-call opener analysis](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-and-worst-cold-call-openers-backed-by-data-from-300m-calls) supports relevant context, transparent identification of a cold call and permission to continue. Its vendor observational data do not establish a universal causal conversion rate; don't promise the reported results for a new campaign.
- [Salesforce: BANT qualification](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/sales/what-is-bant-lead-generation/) describes budget, authority, need and timing, and cautions against rigid checklists and oversimplifying complex buying decisions. The partner and paid-provider distinctions above are adaptations for this workflow.
- [ACMA: telemarketing conduct](https://www.acma.gov.au/telemarketing-compliance-alerts) covers Australian identification, purpose, contact-permission and stop-request requirements. Its guidance also says a published number alone does not establish inferred consent. This is one jurisdiction and one channel, not a complete compliance assessment; consult current authoritative rules when preparing execution elsewhere or through other channels.

Keep research claims separate from campaign facts. Recheck current sources when a task depends on a changing rule or benchmark. Preserve the useful principles without copying entire source materials into outputs.
