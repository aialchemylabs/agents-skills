# Cold Outreach

Turn a defined offer and credible prospect context into a useful first conversation and a clear next step. Support direct customer sales, partners who sell while the user delivers, and paid sales providers as different commercial routes.

## Choose the Work Needed

Use the smallest mode that satisfies the request; combine modes when useful:

- **Research:** produce a sourced, prioritised shortlist and contact routes.
- **Prepare:** draft messages, call openings, qualification questions and follow-ups.
- **Rehearse:** role-play a prospect and give feedback on the user's responses.
- **Review:** assess supplied drafts, transcripts or campaign outcomes and recommend a specific improvement.

For preparation or coaching, read the relevant sections of [the outreach playbook](references/outreach-playbook.md): openings, qualification, objections, follow-up or coaching. Its research notes explain the source limitations. Do not require a full prospect search when the user only needs a script edit or role-play.

## Establish the Campaign Context

Use the conversation and available records before asking for missing information. Identify the offer, intended audience, geography, channel, desired next step, delivery capacity, approved proof and commercial constraints. Resolve only gaps that materially change the work; continue useful preparation while a question is pending.

Keep prospect names, exclusions, pricing, cash constraints and company-specific claims in the campaign context, not in this reusable skill. Carry forward the user's corrections. If the user excludes a company or team, exclude its verified members and routes back into it; do not invent affiliations from names or mutual connections. An existing LinkedIn connection can still be a new commercial approach; label the distinction.

## Research and Prioritise

- Use available authorised business records and current public sources. Prefer purpose-built connectors; use an available browser when the user requests their logged-in LinkedIn or other browser context. This skill does not install tools, provide credentials or grant access.
- If a connector or account is unavailable, name the coverage gap and continue with supplied or public evidence. Never imply a private-source search occurred when it did not.
- Verify current role, company and the specific professional fact used in a draft. Record source links and dates; separate verified facts, user-supplied facts, hypotheses and unknowns. A search with no matches only describes its scope, not the entire relationship history.
- Use relevant professional information. Do not guess email addresses or phone numbers, infer sensitive traits, or treat a published contact detail as consent. Distinguish a verified contact route from permission to use it. A mutual connection is an introduction possibility, not an endorsement.
- For a **customer**, prioritise evidence of the relevant workflow, problem and buying role. For a **sales partner**, look for buyer access, responsibility for closing, delivery fit and plausible economics. For a **paid sales provider**, examine the assigned operator, selling scope, evidence, fees and minimum commitment. Mark an **introducer** separately; introductions alone do not satisfy a request for someone to own sales.
- Use a short evidence-based priority rationale, not fabricated conversion probabilities. A senior title, friendly reply or connection alone is not a qualified opportunity. Do not pad a requested shortlist with weak matches; report the supported subset and the remaining gap.

## Prepare the Conversation

Connect a verified reason for contacting this person to one relevant outcome and one small question. Identify the sender and purpose honestly. Adapt the offer and questions to the commercial route; do not pitch a delivery service to someone the user is seeking to hire for sales.

Keep the first message brief and easy to answer. A call opener should fit roughly 20–30 seconds at a natural pace, but timing and question patterns are coaching guidance, not universal rules. Ask permission, pause and listen. Never imply a prior relationship, manufacture urgency or promise unsupported returns.

Use budget, authority, need and timing as conversational prompts. Record each as supported, unknown or contradicted rather than forcing a binary score. A partner's personal budget is different from an end client's funding; paid provider fees are the user's cost. Check who will actually prospect, qualify, propose, negotiate and close. For employees, frame an authorised company partnership, not undisclosed side work.

Keep internal cash pressure, reservation prices, private correspondence and unapproved customer names out of external drafts. Use approved scope and evidence; make illustrative language visibly provisional. Leave unknown meeting slots as placeholders rather than inventing availability or claiming an invitation was sent.

## Follow-up and Execution Boundaries

Requesting research, drafts or rehearsal does not authorise calls, messages, invitations, CRM writes or paid campaigns. Honour explicit execution authorisation already given; do not request it again solely because this skill was loaded. Before an authorised external action, use an available tool and resolve any material ambiguity about recipient, content or scope. Report only actions actually completed.

For a live campaign, verify applicable channel and jurisdiction requirements using current authoritative guidance when needed. Do not assume a public listing or LinkedIn connection permits contact. Preparation can continue with unresolved execution requirements clearly identified; do not attach a generic compliance exercise to a simple draft edit.

Distinguish a timing objection, a lack of fit, silence and an explicit opt-out. Ask about nurture or a referral only while the person remains willing to engage. If they ask to stop, end the pitch immediately; no final referral request or switch to another channel. Carry the opt-out into campaign notes and future drafts without claiming a CRM update unless one occurred.

## Return a Usable Result

For a prospect report, use the requested format. A useful default table is:

| Priority | Person / current role | Commercial route | Verified fit and sources | Relationship / contact route | Draft message | Qualification gap / next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Keep internal research notes separate from copyable external language. Add call openers, objections and follow-ups only when useful or requested. Summarise coverage gaps and label status accurately: prepared, user-approved, sent, replied, qualified or opted out, supported by the available record. Evidence URLs must support the associated claim; don't attach a generic homepage to an invented detail.

For coaching or review, give the strongest supported observation, a concrete revision and the next practice step. Simulated answers are fictional, never customer evidence. Text alone cannot establish actual vocal pitch, tempo or empathy; evaluate those only from supplied audio or explicit observations.

When analysing results, keep attempts, delivered messages, replies, positive replies, meetings and qualified opportunities distinct. State denominators and time windows; do not infer causal uplift or forecast revenue from a tiny uncontrolled sample. Apply campaign-specific lessons to that campaign rather than silently rewriting the canonical skill.

Broader pipeline management belongs with `sales-and-growth`, neutral research with `run-customer-discovery`, formal proposals with `business-proposal-builder`, and negotiated terms with `prepare-founder-negotiations`, when those skills are available and the user requests that stage. This skill can complete ordinary outreach preparation without them.
