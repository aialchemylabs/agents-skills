## 1. Artifact Selection

Match output to the request.

| Request | Minimum useful artifact |
|---|---|
| Should we pursue? | Decision memo, hard-gate table, evidence gaps, next decision |
| Help shape the opportunity | Buyer map, clarification plan, offer hypotheses, discovery path |
| Plan an RFP response | Compliance matrix, pursuit plan, owners, win themes, content plan, review calendar |
| Review pricing | Buyer-value and seller-economics scenarios, package options, approvals, walk-away triggers |
| Build a statement of work | Delivery boundary, responsibilities, acceptance, dependencies, risks, change control |
| Draft a proposal | Approved content plan plus grounded sections |
| Review a draft | Findings by review gate, corrected claims, blockers, owners, readiness verdict |

Do not produce a long proposal when the decision should be `no-bid`, `shape/clarify`, or `pilot`.

## 2. Content Planning

Plan before prose:

| Section | Evaluator's question | Required answer | Evidence | Compliance ID | Owner | Constraint | Open issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For every scored criterion:

- answer it where the evaluator expects;
- use the buyer's language without copying errors or overclaiming;
- provide proof proportional to the claim;
- make differentiation relevant to the criterion;
- state material dependencies and exceptions clearly;
- respect page, word, format, attachment, and portal constraints.

Use no more than three principal win themes:

```text
BUYER OUTCOME
  because RELEVANT DIFFERENTIATOR
  supported by PROOF
  therefore DECISION SIGNIFICANCE
```

Reject generic themes that a competitor could reuse unchanged.

## 3. Customer-Centered Drafting

Lead with:

1. the buyer's decision and desired outcome;
2. the current cost, constraint, or risk;
3. the proposed path and why it is credible;
4. measurable value and evidence;
5. delivery, adoption, dependencies, and risk reduction;
6. commercial structure and next decision.

Write:

- specific claims rather than superlatives;
- short, evaluable statements;
- outcome before mechanism;
- proof adjacent to the claim;
- scope, assumptions, and dependencies in plain language;
- tables when they make requirements, roles, economics, or options easier to evaluate;
- dates, units, currencies, and definitions consistently.

Avoid:

- brochure-first company history;
- unsupported transformation claims;
- copy-pasted buyer language that does not answer the decision;
- features without connection to outcomes;
- certainty unsupported by evidence;
- hidden exceptions;
- vague next steps;
- "innovative," "seamless," "world-class," "best-in-class," "leading," or "AI-powered" as substitutes for proof.

### Claim test

For every material sentence ask:

1. Is it a fact, attributed claim, estimate, assumption, recommendation, or proposed commitment?
2. What is the source?
3. Is the scope and time period clear?
4. Could an evaluator reasonably misread it as stronger than the evidence?
5. Does an accountable owner approve the commitment?

Remove, qualify, source, or escalate anything that fails.

## 4. Proposal Structures

Adapt structure to the solicitation. Do not override mandatory ordering.

### Unsolicited or shaped proposal

1. Executive decision and outcome
2. Buyer situation and cost of inaction
3. Proposed approach
4. Buyer value and evidence
5. Scope, responsibilities, and acceptance
6. Delivery plan and adoption
7. Team and relevant proof
8. Commercial options
9. Risks, assumptions, and dependencies
10. Decision and next step

### RFP or RFQ response

1. Mandatory forms and declarations
2. Executive response
3. Criterion-by-criterion response in required order
4. Technical and service response
5. Implementation and transition
6. Security, privacy, risk, and compliance
7. Team, governance, and relevant experience
8. Pricing schedules and assumptions
9. Contract departures or clarifications
10. Required attachments

Maintain traceability even when the buyer requires a different final layout.

### Statement of work

1. Purpose and outcomes
2. Scope and deliverables
3. Exclusions
4. Assumptions and dependencies
5. Roles and responsibilities
6. Milestones and schedule
7. Acceptance criteria
8. Fees, expenses, invoicing, and payment
9. Change control and escalation
10. Support, transition, and closeout
11. Risks and required specialist terms

The statement of work must align with the proposal, pricing model, and governing agreement. Escalate contradictions.

### Executive summary logic

An executive summary should let the decision owner answer:

- What decision are we making?
- What outcome will change?
- Why act now?
- Why is this approach credible?
- What proof and economic logic support it?
- What work and risk remain on the buyer?
- What must be approved next?

Do not turn it into a company biography.

## 5. Independent Review Gates

Keep reviews independent enough that prose polish cannot conceal a substantive failure.

### Gate 1: Compliance and submission

Verify:

- every mandatory and scored requirement;
- amendments, Q&A, forms, attachments, signatures, page limits, file names, formats, portal fields, deadline, and time zone;
- exceptions and open clarifications;
- one owner for every incomplete item.

### Gate 2: Customer and evaluator

Test:

- whether the response answers the buyer's decision and criteria;
- whether value, differentiation, and proof are relevant;
- whether the proposal respects the buying process and stakeholder concerns;
- whether the status quo and alternatives are treated credibly;
- whether the buyer can defend the recommendation internally.

### Gate 3: Solution and delivery

Verify:

- feasibility, architecture or operating assumptions, integrations, data, environments, volumes, and performance boundaries;
- deliverables, responsibilities, schedule, dependencies, acceptance, adoption, support, and transition;
- presentation-performance gaps;
- change and escalation paths.

Route deep technical verification to the responsible engineering or domain expert.

### Gate 4: Finance and pricing

Verify:

- formulas, sources, units, currency, tax basis, scenarios, discounts, price authority, cost to serve, margin type, cash timing, and opportunity cost;
- alignment between scope, price, assumptions, payment, acceptance, and risk;
- buyer-value claims and realization assumptions;
- reprice and walk-away triggers.

### Gate 5: Legal, security, privacy, tax, and accounting

Identify the responsible owners and status for:

- contract departures;
- IP, data rights, confidentiality, privacy, security, and regulatory obligations;
- warranties, indemnities, liability, insurance, termination, and dispute terms;
- tax, revenue recognition, financing, employment, or jurisdictional issues.

Do not state specialist approval unless that owner actually provided it.

### Gate 6: Executive approval

Confirm:

- pursuit recommendation;
- strategic fit and capacity;
- price and margin authority;
- delivery owner and resources;
- material exceptions and residual risks;
- negotiation authority and walk-away boundary;
- external claims, credentials, references, and commitments.

### Gate 7: Editorial and production

Verify:

- names, entities, dates, versions, cross-references, numbers, currencies, tables, links, and attachment labels;
- consistent terminology and scope;
- no placeholders, comments, tracked changes, hidden text, or stale examples;
- accessibility and readable structure where applicable;
- final-file integrity and required virus or portal checks.

## 6. Submission and Handoff

Before submission, record:

```text
Approved final file and checksum:
Submission destination:
Deadline and time zone:
Authorized submitter:
Required fields and attachments:
Receipt confirmation method:
Clarification owner:
Negotiation handoff:
Delivery-transition owner:
```

Do not submit without explicit authority. After authorized submission:

- confirm receipt;
- preserve the submitted version;
- log clarifications and buyer communications;
- keep proposal assumptions aligned with negotiation changes;
- transition commitments, risks, and dependencies to delivery;
- conduct a win/loss review using buyer feedback where available.

## 7. Source and Attribution Boundary

This skill synthesizes public concepts; it is not a reproduction of any course, case, teaching note, certification body of knowledge, or proprietary proposal method.

### Harvard and HBS public sources

- William Sahlman, [How to Write a Great Business Plan](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-to-write-a-great-business-plan): use people, opportunity, context, and risk/reward as opportunity questions, not as a customer-proposal template.
- [The HBS Case Method](https://www.hbs.edu/mba/academic-experience/the-case-method): use incomplete-information decision discipline, alternatives, defensible recommendations, and reflection.
- [Entrepreneurial Marketing syllabus](https://online.hbs.edu/documents/entrepreneurial-marketing-syllabus.pdf): use customer value propositions, segmentation, decision-making units, and buying journeys.
- [Business Strategy syllabus](https://online.hbs.edu/documents/business-strategy-syllabus.pdf): use willingness-to-pay, price, cost, willingness-to-sell, value drivers, and implementation as conceptual lenses.
- [Financial Accounting syllabus](https://online.hbs.edu/documents/syllabus-financial-accounting.pdf): use forecasting, cash flow, time value, NPV, IRR, payback, and sensitivity only with defensible inputs.
- Clayton Christensen, [What Customers Want from Your Products](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/what-customers-want-from-your-products): examine the buyer's job and functional, social, and emotional dimensions.
- [Negotiating in Three Dimensions](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/negotiating-in-three-dimensions): distinguish tactics, deal design, and negotiation setup.
- Program on Negotiation, [BATNA Analysis Can Help You Avoid the Agreement Trap](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/batna/too-eager-to-close-nb/): compare the proposed agreement with the best alternative and ignore sunk-cost pressure.
- [How Startups Win Deals by Resetting Expectations](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/karp-confronting-limits-2025): expose delivery limits and create mutual investment rather than overpromise or absorb unbounded customization.

### Proposal-management source

- Association of Proposal Management Professionals, [Winning Business Ecosystem](https://apmp.org/Web/Web/About-Us/Winning-Business-Ecosystem.aspx?hkey=78f94982-b424-49fe-b276-f890f0ab744c): use the public lifecycle as context for qualification, win themes, compliance, proposal planning, independent reviews, submission, negotiation, transition, change control, and learning.

### Attribution rules

- Attribute a named framework when explaining it to the user.
- Paraphrase; do not reproduce paid cases, teaching notes, course content, standards, or certification materials.
- Treat public syllabi and summaries as evidence of topic coverage, not as access to the underlying teaching content.
- Do not put Harvard, HBS, Harvard-level, APMP-certified, affiliated, approved, or endorsed in the skill name, metadata, or marketing.
- Follow the [Harvard policy on use of names and insignias](https://trademark.harvard.edu/policy-on-use-of-harvard-names-and-insignias).
- Do not use institutional logos or insignia.
