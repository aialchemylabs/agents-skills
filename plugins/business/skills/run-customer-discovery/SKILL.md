# Run Customer Discovery

Turn a material business assumption into ethical primary research and decision-grade evidence. Learn how people behave and decide; do not use research as disguised sales or confirmation theater.

## Preserve the Boundary

- Own primary customer and stakeholder discovery, from research design through synthesis.
- Hand broad opportunity scanning to `discover-business-opportunities`.
- Hand a selected venture and its commitment decision to `evaluate-and-shape-venture`.
- Hand critique of an existing plan to `plan-ceo-review`.
- Use a specialist usability workflow when the question is whether people can use a specific interface; combine methods only when both problem and usability evidence are needed.
- Do not claim to have recruited, contacted, interviewed, observed, recorded, or saved research unless that action actually occurred and was authorized.
- Do not hard-code Notion or any other destination. Save or publish only when the user explicitly requests it and the destination is available.

## Load the Field Guide

Read [references/customer-discovery-field-guide.md](references/customer-discovery-field-guide.md) before designing or synthesizing a substantive research round. Use its method-selection rules, templates, evidence ladder, and integrity controls.

## Select the Operating Mode

Choose the smallest mode that satisfies the request:

- **Frame** — convert a decision and assumptions into research questions.
- **Plan** — produce method, recruitment, consent, guide, capture, and analysis plans.
- **Prepare** — create session-ready materials without contacting participants.
- **Conduct** — run a session only through an authorized interaction channel with valid consent and clear recording rules.
- **Synthesize** — analyze supplied notes, recordings, transcripts, artifacts, or observations.
- **Iterate** — update assumptions and design the next evidence round.

State the mode. If the user requests execution but no authorized participant channel or consent process exists, complete the plan and preparation work, then name the blocker.

## Anchor the Research to a Decision

Resolve:

1. the decision, owner, deadline, and resources at risk;
2. the customer, user, buyer, decision maker, operator, beneficiary, and blocker;
3. the current stage and evidence already available;
4. the assumptions that must change the decision;
5. the evidence threshold for support, contradiction, or another round.

Turn assumptions into neutral research questions. Prioritize questions by decision impact and uncertainty. Do not ask participants to resolve strategy questions they cannot know.

## Choose the Evidence Method

Match the method to the question:

- use contextual interviews or observation for current workflow, triggers, workarounds, and consequences;
- use artifact review for concrete evidence of forms, tools, messages, purchases, or handoffs;
- use diary or longitudinal methods for infrequent, distributed, or poorly recalled behavior;
- use surveys for defined population estimates or structured comparisons, with defensible sampling;
- use concept tests for comprehension and relevance, without treating praise as demand;
- use concierge tests, paid pilots, pre-orders, or other ethical behavior experiments for commitment and willingness to pay;
- use existing product or operational data to triangulate what participants report.

Do not use a convenient method merely because it is easy. Interviews are strong for mechanisms and context, not statistical prevalence. Surveys do not explain causality by themselves.

## Recruit for Relevant Experience

Define inclusion and exclusion criteria using role, workflow, decision authority, segment, and recent relevant behavior. Include disconfirming cases such as non-users, churned users, lost buyers, failed adopters, or people who chose the status quo when they can answer the decision.

- Avoid recruiting only friends, advocates, employees, or hand-picked successful users.
- Separate materially different segments instead of averaging them.
- Justify sample scope by method, heterogeneity, decision risk, and the expected value of another session. Do not invoke a universal interview count.
- Make participation voluntary, avoid coercive incentives, support access needs, and disclose conflicts or sponsorship.

## Protect Participants and Research Data

Before a session:

1. explain who is conducting the research, why, what participation involves, and how the material will be used;
2. obtain informed, specific, voluntary consent and separate permission for recording or attributable quotations;
3. collect only information necessary for the research purpose;
4. define storage, access, retention, deletion, withdrawal, and sharing rules;
5. pseudonymize research records where identity is not necessary;
6. follow applicable organizational policy and privacy, employment, health, research, or sector requirements.

Do not place personal, sensitive, confidential, or identifiable information into unapproved tools. Pause for qualified ethics, privacy, legal, safeguarding, or institutional review when research involves vulnerable people, sensitive topics, employees under power imbalance, health information, minors, covert observation, or material risk.

## Run Neutral, Behavior-Focused Sessions

Use a flexible guide rather than a script that suppresses discovery:

1. establish context and role;
2. ask for a specific recent event;
3. reconstruct the workflow step by step;
4. probe triggers, alternatives, workarounds, frequency, cost, delay, risk, and consequences;
5. map who influences, decides, pays, implements, and can block change;
6. explore prior attempts, switching conditions, and reasons to do nothing;
7. inspect relevant artifacts only with permission;
8. test concepts after understanding the current situation;
9. close with clarification, consent-aware follow-up, and next steps.

Ask open, neutral questions. Prefer `Tell me about the last time...` and `What happened next?` over `Would you use...` or `Do you like...`. Do not pitch, teach the participant the desired answer, defend the idea, or count compliments as evidence.

## Capture Traceable Evidence

Record:

- participant code and relevant segment attributes;
- date, method, interviewer, and context;
- observed behavior and specific recent events;
- current alternative, workaround, and switching history;
- friction, consequence, frequency, and decision dynamics;
- direct observation, participant report, researcher interpretation, and open question as separate fields;
- contradictions, negative cases, and potential interviewer or recruitment effects.

Use attributable quotations only within the consent scope. Preserve the source anchor for every synthesis claim without exposing unnecessary identity.

## Synthesize Without Overclaiming

Analyze each session before combining sessions. Then:

1. group evidence by assumption, segment, workflow stage, and decision;
2. compare patterns, mechanisms, contradictions, and outliers;
3. distinguish what participants said from what they did or committed;
4. report descriptive counts only with a denominator and sample context;
5. explain selection bias, missing segments, recall limits, confounds, and researcher influence;
6. update each assumption as `supported`, `weakened`, `contradicted`, or `unresolved`;
7. set confidence from evidence relevance, strength, recency, consistency, and triangulation;
8. recommend the next decision or smallest stronger test.

Never present a small purposive qualitative sample as statistically representative. Do not use `validated` as a blanket label when only one part of an assumption has evidence.

## Deliver the Research Output

For a plan, return:

1. decision and assumptions;
2. research questions and priorities;
3. method and rationale;
4. recruitment and screening criteria;
5. consent, privacy, accessibility, and risk controls;
6. session guide and capture template;
7. analysis method and evidence thresholds;
8. schedule, owner, dependencies, and stop conditions.

For synthesis, return:

1. decision and evidence scope;
2. participants and method, without unnecessary identity;
3. findings with source anchors and confidence;
4. segment differences, contradictions, and negative cases;
5. assumption updates;
6. limitations and unresolved questions;
7. recommendation and next evidence gate;
8. handoff to `evaluate-and-shape-venture` or another relevant workflow.

Keep findings tied to the decision. A polished report is not a substitute for an honest account of evidence quality.
