# Methodology

## Frame the target and calendar

Establish the decision, units, series IDs, target, timestamps/timezone, completed-period convention, publication cutoff and revision limitations. Observed zeros are observations; blanks/nulls are missing. Stockout-constrained sales do not identify unconstrained demand. Stocks and ratios cannot be added as flow totals. Driver-based cash planning, statements, runway and controls stay with `startup-finance-and-controls`; historical statistical forecasts can inform that work without taking accounting ownership.

Use explicit dates. Resolve “next quarter” as fiscal/calendar before proposing. History ending 2027-02-28 with calendar Q2 requested requires 122 daily generated steps (March–June), of which 91 (April–June) are reported/scored. Backtests reproduce the same 31-step bridge and reporting lead, not an easier immediate 91-step horizon. Do not change cadence to fit model limits. Monthly automatic dates are month-end; other calendars use a supplied ordered calendar. Hourly timestamps span DST in absolute hours; ambiguous local hours require offsets. Daily dates advance in the supplied timezone. Exclude incomplete observations explicitly; the parser rejects `complete=false` instead of treating the period as finished.

Cadence must be regular, including explicit missing rows, or use a supplied calendar. Inspection reports coverage/missingness/zeros/negative values; six monthly records and six months of daily values are different evidence, with no universal minimum establishing accuracy. Short history may warrant an exploratory statistical option or a data-collection recommendation.

## Information and evaluation

All related series share calendar and as-of boundaries. `available_at` denotes when a target observation became usable; absent metadata means immediate availability is an assumption, with revisions unknown. For historical backtests use actual historical vintages where possible; today's revised data cannot establish real-time performance. Never describe this limitation as solved by a timestamp alone.

Preparation is fold-local: retain the dated grid through the origin, truncate to the selected context, mask unpublished values, then fill missing training values with that fold's available observed mean. Publication delays never remove calendar positions. Historical covariates use the same row-availability mask and their own fold-local mean; independently published covariates require an explicit prepared snapshot. No interpolation from the holdout, automatic season detection, fitted transformation or external normalization is used. Adapter normalization runs only on the resulting training arrays. Context truncation and this imputation are part of the option. Cross-learning is off by default. If selected, Chronos groups must fit a single fixed batch including covariates; changing batching changes information-sharing semantics.

Covariates are numeric: past-only, known-future, as-of forecast vintages, or explicit scenarios. Supply historical columns and timestamped future values, each available at the origin. For each origin/date the latest available vintage is used. Backtests require historical values/vintages/scenarios too; do not use realized future actuals as if known. Known calendars may legitimately be available far in advance. Scenario forecasts are conditional, not causal effects. The TimesFM adapter excludes covariates and XReg.

Select explicit chronological origins, a horizon-matched scoring mask, a primary loss and weights before running. A final untouched holdout is preferable where feasible; this CLI's rolling-origin comparisons are labelled exploratory and overlapping origins are disclosed. Record failures/missing observations separately. Every method is scored on the same nonmissing targets, then compared only on common successful series/origin folds. An adapter failure cannot benefit from easier folds.

MAE/RMSE retain units. MASE uses the mean absolute selected-season training difference; zero/absent denominators are undefined, never forced to zero. Per-series primary losses are averaged across folds weighted by scored observation counts, then pooled by explicit positive series weights. This is a deliberate mean of series/fold losses (including RMSE), not a global RMSE from pooled residuals. Use MASE for unlike units; do not pool raw currency, tickets and power errors. Report counts and excluded folds along with rankings.

## Statistics, uncertainty and totals

TimesFM's loaded primary output is channel 5/q50, a median. Chronos returns a median in a variable named `mean`; the adapter tests equality to q50 at runtime. TimesFM channel 0 is not exposed as a verified mean. Never invent a mean from sparse quantiles. Both use float32 CPU, no torch compilation, no inferred nonnegative clipping and rejection of nonfinite quantiles. Crossed quantiles are rejected by default or clamped about q50 when that correction was explicitly selected; the same policy runs inside every backtest. TimesFM uses fold-local normalization, flip invariance and its continuous quantile head; internal context/horizon patch padding is explicit in the runtime reference.

q10–q90 is an 80% **native nominal, uncalibrated** interval. Report observed coverage as covered/count over scored targets and pinball losses, not a claim of calibration or future coverage. No post-hoc interval fitting is implemented.

For same-unit flows choose `direct-total` (forecast a complete aggregate historical series) or `sum-point-rules` (sum component point outputs, labelled precisely and evaluated on historical totals). Direct-total currently excludes covariates and cross-learning. Neither sum-of-medians nor a sum of generic reference rules is an aggregate mean/median. Aggregate intervals are unavailable: never sum marginal quantiles. Period point sums are likewise labelled point sums, with no claimed interval or expected value. Compatible verified means could support mean summation in a future adapter; neither current adapter advertises one. Stocks/ratios require a separately authorized valid transformation or aggregation definition rather than these flow totals.
